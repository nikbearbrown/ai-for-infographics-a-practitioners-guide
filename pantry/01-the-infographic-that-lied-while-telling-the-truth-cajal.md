## Fig 1.1 — Same Data, Two Claims: 4× Told as 16×
- **Concept:** The data-accuracy vs perceptual-accuracy gap made literal. The same true 4:1 ratio (8 vs 2 per 1,000) encoded two ways — honest bars on a common baseline (reads 4:1) and diameter-scaled circles (reads 16:1 because area goes as r²). The measurement error IS the point.
- **Type:** Side-by-side before/after comparison panel — bars left, circles right, each annotated with the ratio the eye actually reads.
- **Shows:** Left panel: two bars, lengths 4:1, "READS 4× — HONEST." Right panel: two circles, diameters 4:1 / areas 16:1, with the red ring on the oversized circle and "READS 16× — THE LIE."
- **Scope:** Chapter 1 opening (the proportional-circles failure) and the "data accuracy vs perceptual accuracy" section. Anchors the worked example without duplicating its SVG code.
- **Red-accent element:** The over-large circle's area exaggeration on the misleading (right) panel — the single distorted structural claim.
