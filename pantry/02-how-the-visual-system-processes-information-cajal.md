## Fig 2.1 — The Cleveland–McGill Channel-Accuracy Ladder
- **Concept:** The experimental ranking of elementary perceptual tasks by accuracy — position on a common scale (most accurate) down through length, angle, area, to color hue (least accurate). The rule: match the channel to the precision the task demands.
- **Type:** Vertical ranked ladder, seven rungs, each rung carrying a tiny inline glyph demonstrating the channel and an accuracy-error band that widens downward.
- **Shows:** Seven rungs top-to-bottom (position-common-scale → position-non-aligned → length → angle/slope → area → volume/curvature → color hue), error widening as you descend; the area rung flagged as where the Chapter 1 circles sit.
- **Scope:** Chapter 2 "The channel hierarchy: why length beats area." The ladder is referenced repeatedly; this gives it a single canonical visual.
- **Red-accent element:** The top rung — position on a common scale, the most accurate channel — the rung the corrected bar chart climbs to.

## Fig 2.2 — The Legend Tax: Lookup Table vs. Direct Label
- **Concept:** A legend is a lookup table the reader must hold in working memory and re-fetch on every eye movement; direct labeling deletes the table and returns capacity to the comparison (spatial contiguity, Mayer).
- **Type:** Before/after two-panel — left: chart bands + a separated legend with shuttle arrows; right: the same bands with labels in place.
- **Shows:** Left panel: color swatches in a legend, dashed back-and-forth "re-fetch" shuttle arrows to the bands, "+6 working-memory bindings." Right panel: labels sitting on the bands, "0 lookups — contiguity satisfied."
- **Scope:** Chapter 2 cognitive-load section, "The legend deserves a moment." Makes the re-fetch cost concrete.
- **Red-accent element:** The shuttle arrows on the left panel — the manufactured extraneous load that the fix removes.
