# CAJAL — Chapter 9: Annotation: When It Earns Its Place

## Figure 9.1 — The four-job test
- **Concept:** Annotation earns its place only when it does one of four jobs — Interpretation, Orientation, Context, Caveat — applied ruthlessly so most candidate text gets cut.
- **Type:** Decision filter / four-gate diagram.
- **Shows:** Candidate annotations entering the test; four labeled gates (Interpretation = highest value, then Orientation, Context, Caveat); a "CUT" bin for text that does no needed job (the axis-restater, the over-labeled points, decorative text).
- **Scope:** One filter pass; one accent.
- **Red accent:** The single INTERPRETATION sentence ("the gap widens fastest after 2008") — the one annotation that most earns its place.
