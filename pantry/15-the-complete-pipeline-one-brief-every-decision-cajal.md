# CAJAL — Chapter 15 figures

## Fig 15-01 — The complete pipeline: agent execution vs. the human-judgment spine
- **Concept:** The whole pipeline (Design → Audit → Generate → Verify → Handoff) in one view, split into two columns: what the agent does (execution) and what only the human can do (the design-intelligence layer). The right column is the irreducible spine.
- **Type:** Two-track flow / swimlane (agent track parallel to human-judgment track across five phases).
- **Shows:** Five phases left-to-right; top lane = agent execution (inventory, render, extract, rasterize); bottom spine = the three+ irreducibly-human-judgment points (decide what counts as a gap / quarantine; axis-zero, color ceiling, the accent; ship/no-ship; the imported position is wrong). The three marked human-judgment points highlighted.
- **Scope:** Single panel; phases as columns, two horizontal lanes, the human spine running beneath.
- **Red accent (ONE):** The human-judgment spine (the connected run of human-decision points) — the book's thesis made visual.

## Fig 15-02 — The production package: every artifact traces to a decision
- **Concept:** "Done" is not a pretty PNG; it is a package where every artifact traces back to a decision and every decision back to the brief. DISCLOSURE.md and DEFENSE.md are the evidence the human owned the judgment column.
- **Type:** Traceability tree (brief at root, artifacts as leaves, decisions as the connecting edges).
- **Shows:** BRIEF.md at the root; the package files (scope.md, DESIGN.md, CLAUDE.md, PROJECT.md, .svg, .ai, .png, DEFENSE.md, DISCLOSURE.md) as nodes; edges labeled by the decision each traces to; the derived .png shown downstream of the .svg source.
- **Scope:** Single panel; brief at top/left, package fanning out, traceability arrows.
- **Red accent (ONE):** DISCLOSURE.md — the artifact that names the three human-judgment decisions, the package's proof of authorship.
