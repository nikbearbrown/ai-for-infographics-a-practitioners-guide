   05: Storyboarding
   Putting Your Idea on Paper
   Choose Images to Support Messaging
            The Storyboarding Step
            This is where you will begin to visualize your infographic on paper. Don’t worry about your
            drawing skills. This is important!

      You can now think about your initial visualization of your infographic by

   developing a storyboard or a sketch of your idea(s). Remember to pull out the parts

   of the story you developed (i.e., Act I, Act II, Act III) to ensure that your key

   messages are represented. Don’t worry about your artistic skill level as you are just

   brainstorming. Try to think how you’d tell your story if you couldn’t communicate

   except with visuals; you can then add in some interesting facts or a few sentences of

   text as needed to tell your story (in a storyboard you can just put a placeholder for

   text).

   07: Constructing Continued
      You can now start building your infographic based on your storyboard you
   created in step 5. We are using PowerPoint for the workshop because everyone has it
   and has some familiarity with it and its functions. PowerPoint has a limit for slide
   size. To optimize for the largest size and for a vertical layout, we suggest you resize
   your slide to be 14 in wide by 56 in high. You can also use other programs as well.
   Other possible programs include Microsoft Publisher, and Adobe Photoshop and
   Illustrator.
      Remember to be consistent in your graphic styles. Don’t worry if you have to
   deviate from your storyboard once you start building the electronic version of your
   infographic. Please feel free to use any of the templates provided for you on your
   workshop jumpdrives as well as any of the graphics NEDARC created for you and
   located in the file called NEDARC graphics library.pptx

       Keep it Concise and Simple
         Every meaningful idea can be expressed in one
         sentence. A short sentence. If you can’t do it, you
         don’t understand it. Rewrite it until you can’t
         subtract any more words. If you are still left with a
         lot of text, it may be because you don’t think
         infographics work. Then be honest with yourself and
         write an article instead. Don’t hide your article
         inside an infographic - it’s obvious. If you are
         making the graphic, most of the text has to go.
                                     - Anna Vital, Information Designer

   09: Sharingi
   Post or Share Electronically
   Advertise/Market Your Infographic

         The Sharing Step
        This is it! This is the last step. You will want to make your infographic available to the public or your
        intended audience. You will also need to decide how to let them know it is available.




                     The more complex your infographic, the larger it’s likely to be from a file size point of
                     view. Because infographics are intended to be shared and viewed online, file size is
                     important for viewing purposes. You’ll drive viewers away if your graphic is so big that it
                     doesn’t load easily.
                     Keep your graphic as close to 1MB as possible.
   Saving Your PowerPoint Infographic as an Image . . .
   Once the infographic is considered final (if using PowerPoint), Go to the File tab. Choose Save As. Select
   PNG Portable Network Graphics Format (*.png) under the Save as type. Select Save. Now your
   infographic is a high quality image that can be shared.




                       Search Engine Optimization (SEO) is a process of using words and terms for online
                       materials and websites in such a way that Internet search engines (such as Google) find
                       your material quickly to increase the amount of visitors.


                      How you name your infographic directly impacts when it comes to SEO. Search engines
                      send out search bots routinely, looking for more and better information. At some point,
                      they will find your infographic on your website. Based on search bots’ sets of rules,
                      search engines in turn decide where your infographic will show up in search results.

   Using Appropriate Keywords . . .
   What you don’t want to do is cram your infographic so full of keywords that the search engines label it as
   SPAM. What you want to do is use keywords in the name of the file that are important to you from an
   SEO point of view.

                             Ways to increase SEO 1:
                               • Put the keyword in the domain name (e.g., emsc.com) if applicable
               SEO             • Put the keyword in the post URL
                               • Use at least one keyword in the title tag. Create a filename so it reads as a natural
                                   statement, not just a collection of keywords.
                               • Make sure your post about your infographic post uses keywords




                           Here are a couple ways to signal to your audience that the content is an infographic:

                                     Put the word Infographic in the title.

                                     Include an introductory paragraph for the infographic. Including an introductory
                                     paragraph also helps internet search engines validate your post and identify it as
                                     meaningful content.

                    Your goal for your infographic is for it to go viral. In terms of communication and sharing,
                    influences are those people who already have a media presence and can help you share
                    your infographic.

                    How to identity and find key influencers for your topic? Conduct a Google search on your
   topic and find media outlets (e.g., newspapers, online reporting sites, blogs) individuals such as reporters
   who have reported on the topic in the past. Not all media outlets will be interested in your infographic or
   message. To help you identify the right ones, it’s important to know your audience. Where would your
   audience want to go for the type of information in your infographic?

   One tool that can further help you identify influencers is a website called Topsy.com. Topsy.com is a social
   analytics tool that will also allow you to identify influencers on various topics. Here’s how to get started
   with Topsy:

      1. Go to www.topsy.com.
      2. Type in a keyword related to your infographic in the search bar. You’ll get a list of content related to

          your keyword. Note (on the left side) the Influencers icon                  . This is who Topsy
          believes are the best influencers on the topic you typed into the search bar. They are the most likely
          to tweet about your infographic topic.

   Once you have identified a media outlet, you can copy and paste their website URL into sites like Compete
   (www.compete.com) and Alexa Internet (www.alexa.com). These sites will tell you you how much traffic
   the media outlet’s website receive.



   Using Pinterest for Getting the Word Out . . .
              Pinterest (www.pinterest.com) can be another good place for you to get the word out about your
              infographics. The first step is finding out which Pinterest boards are named by your
              topic/infographic topic. Here’s a cool trick you can use in

      1. Type in the following: site:pinterest.com inurl:dogs. Note the space between site:pinterest.com and
         inurl:dogs.
      2. Next, try this one: site:pinterest.com inurl:infographics. Each time, you simply just add in the
         topic you want to search for after inurl:. By doing this, you can see who is making boards about your
         topic.
      3. Reach out to those people who run the board and see whether they’ll pin your infographic to their
         board.
